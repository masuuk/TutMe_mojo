Questions

Before you start

Which part should I start with?
Working surveyors usually start with Part 1 — GNSS Surveying — since frames and calibration pay off immediately. If your problem is specifically grids and projections, Part 2 stands alone; it re-derives the ellipsoid facts it needs and cross-links back where it borrows from Part 1.
How do the two parts connect?
Part 1 ends with geodetic coordinates ready for mapping; Part 2 opens at exactly that seam and derives the projection that finishes the job. Part 2 closes by handing the projection back into Part 1’s field-to-office workflow — site calibration, georeferencing, adjustment.
Why n instead of eccentricity-squared expansions?
Series in the third flattening n converge roughly twice as fast as classic e² forms. Six terms of the Kruger series hold sub-millimetre accuracy across a full 6° UTM zone.
What about production use?
Learn it here, ship it with the libraries — GeographicLib for the projection (the batch GIS section shows where each hand-rolled routine matches it), and your receiver’s RTK engine for the GNSS side, now with the residuals to audit it.
Which ellipsoids and datums are covered?
WGS84 throughout, with the parameter-derivation sections written so any ellipsoid — GRS80, Clarke 1880 for LO systems — drops in by changing two numbers.
